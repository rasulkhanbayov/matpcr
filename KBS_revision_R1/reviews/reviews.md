## Reviewer 1

**R1.summary** This paper evaluates how reliably language models interpret materials data. It checks their numerical claims and property judgments against physical constraints and computational references, then uses feedback from these checks to revise their answers. The topic is relevant to AI-assisted scientific analysis, and I appreciate the Authors’ open discussion of limitations and negative results. My main concerns are whether the benchmark adequately evaluates scientific reasoning and whether the reported gains lead to more accurate scientific answers. I recommend major revision with a few comments below.

**R1.M1** Checking individual claims is not the same as evaluating a reasoning chain. Each item contains only one checkable claim (p. 18), which limits the support for the paper’s central claim about reasoning-chain consistency. The accuracy–consistency comparison also partly reflects different tolerances or definitions of stability (p. 8–9). These differences explain why the scores disagree, but do not show that PCR captures reasoning errors beyond those measured by answer accuracy. A clearer account of what PCR adds to scientific evaluation is needed.

**R1.M2** The checks need stronger scientific validation. Detecting all planted violations is useful, but does not tell us how many errors are missed in real responses. Reviewing only flagged cases cannot answer this question. The XRD precision and the ranking reversal under different tolerances also suggest that the results depend substantially on the chosen criteria (p. 25). Validation against measurement uncertainty and independent scientific judgment would make the assessment more convincing. The theoretical guarantee should remain explicitly conditional.

**R1.M3** The interpretation of self-verification gains needs more evidence. Higher PCR shows that revised answers satisfy the checks more often, since the same checks provide feedback and score the outcome. Zero regressions also follow from the stopping rule (Algorithm 1). The Authors acknowledge that improved consistency need not mean improved correctness. However, the stronger conclusion that most gains represent “retreat” (p. 10) is not supported by final accuracy being lower than PCR alone. Paired changes in correctness would help show what the revisions actually achieve.

**R1.M4** Coverage and sampling complicate the model comparisons. The proportion of checkable responses changes with prompt wording (p. 25), and the stability samples contain very few stable structures (p. 9). Some score differences may therefore come from which responses are evaluated and which materials are sampled. The comparisons would be more reliable if coverage, class balance and aggregation choices were accounted for explicitly.

**R1.m1** It is unclear how the information supplied in the DFT tasks identifies the corresponding reference structure (p. 16). This matters because structures with the same composition can have different properties.

**R1.m2** The verifier’s pooled performance gives limited insight into its usefulness for a particular scientific task (p. 11, lines 11–24). Results within individual constraint families would help clarify this.

## Reviewer 2

**R2.summary** The manuscript introduces MatPCR, a label-free benchmark that scores the physical consistency of a model's reasoning chain rather than its final answer, using six programmatic oracle families over diffraction, microscopy, Raman spectra and Materials Project-grounded property checks, and it releases three artifacts: the Physical-Consistency Rate (PCR) with bootstrap intervals and an exact oracle-bias identity, a fine-tuned violation verifier, and Constraint-Grounded Self-Verification. The topic fits Knowledge-Based Systems, the central idea (physics as the label; oracles as necessary conditions; no human annotation of correct answers) is crisp and useful, the oracle-validity gate is run before any model is evaluated and reported in unusual detail, the accuracy-versus-consistency analysis is the strongest part of the paper, and the negative results are reported rather than buried.However, the manuscript still has a number of unresolved problems and does not meet the standard required for acceptance in its present form, so I recommend major revision. The specific concerns and the actions I am asking for are set out in Section 3.

**R2.S1** (strength) The analysis of how oracle error displaces the reported rate is exact and makes no distributional assumption, giving an identity for the displacement, a one-sided guarantee when the oracle is complete, and a bound for substituting a learned verifier for the oracle. It states precisely what the validity gate must establish and is candid about the bound holding only within the fitted distribution.

**R2.S2** (strength) The measurement design separates constructs: accuracy criteria are defined independently of each oracle's tolerance, consistency and correctness are treated as two axes, and balanced accuracy exposes a base-rate confound that raw accuracy hides. The demonstration that the metric is not answer accuracy under another name is the clearest scientific contribution of the paper.

**R2.S3** (strength) The self-verification experiment isolates a mechanism rather than showing an effect: comparison is paired on identical items, regressions are reported alongside fixes, the tests are corrected for multiplicity, and matched equal-computation controls do not reproduce the gain. The paper also states that the improvement is mostly retreat rather than correction.

**R2.W1** The manuscript narrates its own internal revision history, and a reader cannot verify it. The oracle-validity and model-evaluation passages of the Results state that the reported values replace an earlier pass, the Discussion revisits a claim the authors have withdrawn, and the running text carries internal labels and repository paths, including the first-principles evaluation label in the caption of Figure 4, the experiment numbering in the Acknowledgements, and the code and result-file names in the Statistics paragraph of the Methods. Please report only final-protocol results in the main text, move the audit trail into a short note in the supplementary material, define or delete the internal labels, and remove repository paths and internal file names from the prose.

**R2.W2** The manuscript gives incompatible accounts of which oracle version each precision audit was judged against. The error-taxonomy paragraph of the Methods and the note under Figure 1c exclude the spectrum and first-principles families because their human review predates the final oracle definitions, whereas the caption of Table 1, the spectra-precision paragraph of the Results and the statement that both revised oracles were re-collected report those same audits as performed under the corrected definitions. The precision values in Table 1 are the warrant for every rate the paper reports, so the inconsistency is not editorial. Please add a short table giving, for each oracle family, the versions used to sample and to judge the audited cases, the date of the audit and the number of cases, and align these passages with it.

**R2.W3** Every precision figure in Table 1 comes from a single annotator. No agreement statistic is reported, the cases that annotator was unsure about are excluded from the ratio, and several families were audited on small samples. These figures justify treating the oracles as valid and therefore underpin every rate in the paper. Please have a second independent annotator re-label a subsample of the audited cases in each family, report agreement between the annotators, add interval estimates alongside the point estimates, and add a sensitivity analysis that counts unsure cases first as violations and then as clean cases.

**R2.W4** The model comparisons are not paired and are not adjusted for multiplicity. Items are drawn afresh for each model in the protocol paragraph of the Methods, so the models are never compared on a common item set; the comparative claims, including the statement that six of the other seven models exceed the local model, rest on whether intervals overlap rather than on a test of difference; and the tolerance-sensitivity and prompt-paraphrase paragraphs of the Methods both report that the diffraction ranking reverses under reasonable changes. Please evaluate all models on one common and paired item set, replace interval-overlap reasoning with paired item-level tests corrected across the family of comparisons, report effect sizes with the intervals, and mark the diffraction comparisons as provisional or remove them from the headline claims.

**R2.W5** The share of items the metric actually covers is never reported. The metric and the passage on exposed constraints restrict the rate to items whose chain states a checkable quantity, the record counts behind Table 2 and the caption of Figure 3a show that this subset varies in size across cells, and the prompt-sensitivity paragraph notes that the checkable rate changes with the wording. Because items that state no quantity are excluded rather than penalised, a model can raise its score by saying less. Please report the checkable share for every model and constraint type in the main evaluation, define how non-checkable chains are treated, and state how the ordering changes under each convention.

**R2.W6** Differences that are small relative to the sampling uncertainty are discussed as substantive findings. The tier and generational conclusions in the Discussion rest on margins no larger than the confidence intervals reported for Table 2, and one of them compares a single pair of models, the flagship and balanced tiers of the same series. Please state the smallest difference the design can detect, report effect sizes together with the intervals, and either bound these claims explicitly or move them to future work.

**R2.W7** The related-work coverage should be extended beyond the benchmark tradition the paper criticises. The manuscript positions itself against four contemporaneous benchmarks, but it cites little recent work on how image-language models are evaluated outside final-answer scoring, on chain-of-thought reasoning and its faithfulness, or on how domain knowledge is infused and checked in large language models in other fields. Please add and critically discuss the following recent studies, and position each of them as conceptual or background context rather than as a comparator for the metric: Seeing Sarcasm Through Different Eyes: Analyzing Multimodal Sarcasm Perception in Large Vision-Language Models, which analyses the perception of an image-language model beyond benchmark scores; Cot-evo: Evolutionary distillation of chain-of-thought for scientific reasoning, which improves scientific reasoning chains rather than evaluating them; Faithcot-bench: Benchmarking instance-level faithfulness of chain-of-thought reasoning, which tests whether a stated chain reflects the underlying computation, the concern raised in your Introduction; and Legal knowledge infusion for large language models: A survey, which shows how domain knowledge is injected and checked in a specialised field. For each, state how it relates to the keywords of this submission, namely materials informatics, multimodal models, scientific reasoning, physics-grounded evaluation and model verification, and how the present work differs from it.
