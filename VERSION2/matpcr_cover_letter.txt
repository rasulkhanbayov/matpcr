Dear Editors of npj Computational Materials,

We submit our manuscript, "Physics Is the Label: A Label-Free Benchmark and Verifier
for the Physical Consistency of Materials Reasoning in Vision-Language and Language
Models," for consideration as an Article.

Context and importance. Vision-language and language models are increasingly used
to interpret materials data, but they are evaluated almost entirely on whether the
final answer matches a human label, and a recent wave of discovery agents instead
verifies final proposals or the correctness of DFT execution. None of this measures
whether the model's reasoning itself respects the physics that materials data fixes,
and none of it yields a reusable tool the community can run. This matters because a
reasoning chain that misreads a scale bar, assigns a diffraction peak to an
impossible angle, or calls an above-hull structure stable is unreliable inside any
analysis or screening pipeline, regardless of whether its final answer happens to be
right.

What we contribute. We introduce a DFT-grounded, label-free way to measure and
improve the physical consistency of materials reasoning: programmatic and
first-principles oracles that require no human annotation, the Physical-Consistency
Rate metric, a label-free agentic self-verification loop, and an open released
verifier. The approach applies to both image and structure or text inputs and is
built entirely from public data, making it broadly reusable.

Fit for the journal. The work is computational, reproducible, and provides an open
artifact for the computational-materials community, consistent with the journal's
scope and emphasis on tools and methods that others can adopt.

We confirm that this manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere and that there
is no significant overlap with other work by the authors under consideration or in
press. In accordance with the journal's policy, our use of an AI assistant to help
draft text, parts of the method, and code is disclosed in the Methods; all content
was independently verified by the authors, who take full responsibility, and no AI
system is an author.

Related work (per submission policy). The following related works by overlapping authors are published, in press, or under review; copies can be provided to the Editor on request: CrysMTM (Machine Learning: Science and Technology, 2025); R-CRYSTALS (under review); QuantumShellNet (Computational Materials Science, 2025); HalluVerse25 (preprint); and IRIS (preprint). MatPCR does not overlap in content with these: it measures and corrects the physical consistency of model reasoning with label-free, DFT-grounded oracles, rather than providing a dataset, a generation benchmark, a property-prediction model, or a text-factuality benchmark.
